Footprints on Mount Merbabu, Last Memories Before We Part Ways.

15 Nov 2026 • By Ekuivalen
Join The Newsletter for Tech & Builders
1,200+ Readers
Ekuivalen

Some moments deserve more than a casual goodbye at a café or a quick message in a group chat. When we realized that our routines were about to pull us in different directions, we wanted one last shared experience that none of us would forget. That was how four of us ended up packing our bags and heading toward Mount Merbabu.

This climb wasn’t just about ticking off the 3,142-meter summit from a bucket list. It was about celebrating our last stretches of time together before everyone stepped into their own chapters—and discovering how easily physical exhaustion turns into laughter when you are walking beside the right people.

Why We Chose the Mountain

Goodbyes usually feel rushed. Everyone gets caught up in plans, deadlines, and preparations for what comes next. Going up a mountain forces you to slow down. Up there, there is no rushing past the moment—just the trail ahead, the weight on your shoulders, and hours of uninterrupted time to actually talk.

We didn’t need anything overly planned. We just needed a challenge we had to finish together, where nobody got left behind and every break along the slope became another story to laugh about later.

Heavy Steps, Light Conversations

The first part of the climb always starts with high energy—jokes flying around, confident paces, and plenty of photos. Then the incline starts doing its job. Legs get heavier, breaths grow shorter, and the distance between rest stops suddenly feels twice as long as it looked on the map.

What kept us moving wasn’t stamina alone, but the small things that happened along the way:

  • Constant banter on steep climbs: Whenever someone started complaining about the slope, someone else immediately turned it into a joke just to keep the mood up.
  • Matching each other’s pace: Reaching the top meant nothing if we didn’t get there as a group, so we waited, shared water, and pushed through together.
  • Simple breaks that felt priceless: Sitting on the dirt trail catching our breath with open mountain views around us felt better than any comfortable chair back in the city.
The soreness in your legs fades within a few days, but the laughter you shared while trying to catch your breath on a steep trail stays with you for years.

Cold Air and Unfiltered Talks

As the elevation climbed and the temperature dropped, the noise of everyday life felt miles away. Wrapped in jackets against the mountain wind, our conversations naturally shifted from silly jokes to honest reflections—old memories we used to laugh at, plans we were nervous about, and where we hoped to see ourselves in the next few years.

There is something about sitting together in the cold after hours of climbing that makes people more open. Even a simple warm drink and quick snacks tasted unusually good, simply because we had earned them together.

Standing at Triangulation Peak (3,142 MDPL)

The final push toward the summit tested whatever energy we had left. Every ridge felt like the last one—until another climb appeared right behind it. Still, step by step, the horizon kept opening wider until we finally reached Puncak Triangulasi at 3,142 MDPL.

Standing beside the green summit sign under the bright sky, all the fatigue suddenly felt worth it. Our faces showed the dust and exhaustion from the climb, yet none of us could stop smiling. Taking that group photo at the top wasn’t just proof that we made it—it was a snapshot of friendship right before life carried us onto separate roads.

What We Brought Back Down

Walking down the mountain always feels bittersweet. With every step back toward the base, reality creeps closer: new environments, busy schedules, and the quiet truth that gathering all four of us in one place won’t always be this easy anymore.

Still, we didn’t come home empty-handed. We left our footprints on Mount Merbabu and brought back a memory solid enough to outlast distance. Whenever we eventually cross paths again, we’ll always have 3,142 meters of sweat, wind, and laughter to look back on.
